About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural and Applied Economics at Texas Tech University. My work covers consumer behavior, agricultural markets, and international trade, and I use econometrics and machine learning to answer practical questions in agriculture.

I have lived and worked in both the United States and Latin America. Before graduate school I managed commercial melon fields in Guatemala and worked in quality control for fresh produce in Florida. That experience shapes the questions I ask about producers, consumers, and global food systems. My newer work looks at the economics of AI and precision technologies in agriculture and ranching: what they are worth to a business, what keeps producers from adopting them, and where they save labor or reduce risk.

Job market paper

Forecasts through 2028

Detecting Potential Export Opportunities for the U.S. Meat Industry: A Machine Learning Approach

with Modhurima Amin and Syed Badruddoza

This paper forecasts U.S. beef, pork, and chicken exports through 2028 using long short-term memory (LSTM) and artificial neural network (ANN) models, and benchmarks them against a Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood (PPML) gravity model. The forecasts show which destination markets are likely to grow and how much accuracy machine learning adds over a standard trade model.
